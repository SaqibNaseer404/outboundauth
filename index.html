Questions, answered

Straight answers on email authentication and deliverability — no jargon.

Why are my emails going to spam if I'm not a spammer?

Spam filters don't know you're legitimate — they judge signals. The most common technical cause is broken or missing email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC). Beyond that, sender reputation, email content, and list quality all play a role. Start with the free audit tools above: authentication is the fastest thing to check and fix.

Do I need SPF, DKIM, and DMARC — or is one enough?

You need all three. SPF authorizes which servers may send for your domain, DKIM proves messages are genuine and unaltered, and DMARC ties them together with a policy for handling failures. Gmail and other major providers increasingly expect all three, especially from bulk senders.

Will fixing authentication guarantee inbox placement?

Honestly: no, but it's the foundation everything else builds on. Authentication gets you past the technical filters; inbox placement also depends on your sender reputation, content, and how recipients engage with your emails. Fix authentication first — it's the quickest win — then work on the rest.

Do these tools change my DNS records?

No. The tools analyze records you paste in and tell you what's wrong in plain language. Any changes happen in your own DNS panel (or your domain registrar), so you're always in control.

How long does it take to fix deliverability?

Fixing the records themselves takes minutes; DNS changes usually propagate within a few hours. Rebuilding a damaged sender reputation takes longer — days to weeks of consistent, clean sending. That's why catching authentication problems early matters.

How is this different from hiring an outbound agency?

Agencies run your entire outbound system — lists, copy, sending, iteration — typically for $500+/month. OutboundAuth is the affordable path: free diagnostics and guides so you can fix things yourself, plus one-time expert help (audit, full setup, monitoring) when you want a specialist to handle the authentication side.

Results are guidance, not a replacement for testing with your sending provider.
